MonoDebris: Augmented Dexterity for Surgical Debridement via On-the-fly Calibration and Monocular Attention Windows

MonoDebris demonstrates an augmented dexterity pipeline for autonomous surgical debridement using a cable-driven robot with monocular vision and on-the-fly calibration.

Abstract

Automating surgical subtasks such as debridement (removal of diseased or dead tissue fragments) with a cable-driven robotic system could free surgeons from tedious and repetitive procedures, while remaining challenging due to imprecision in spatial perception and cable actuation. In this work, we present an augmented dexterity pipeline for surgical debridement using an under-calibrated cable-driven robot equipped with an RGB camera. We perform on-the-fly calibration to compensate for robotic kinematic and camera-robot transformation errors during operation, and use monocular attention windows to enable interactive perception for fragment grasping without the need of accurate depth information. Across 100 physical trials, on-the-fly calibration for gripper alignment in the image space showed that the average gripper position offset was reduced from 37 to fewer than 5 pixels within 4 optimization steps, taking an average online optimization time of only 0.39s. Physical experiments involving 100 single-fragment removals achieved an average success rate of 93%, while five-fragment removals resulted in a 70% success rate, rising to 95% with one human intervention. MonoDebris outperformed previous procedural baselines and State-Of-The-Art (SOTA) policies, with improvement rates over the second-best method reaching 48% and 26%, respectively. It demonstrated an average removal time of 11 seconds per fragment, translating to an effective throughput of 304 fragment removals per hour. Extending the evaluation to 400 fragment removals suggests consistent performance, yielding a 92.25% success rate.

Key Contributions

  • On-the-fly Calibration: Dynamic gripper alignment approach that corrects inaccuracies in kinematics and camera-robot transformation of cable-driven robot in the image space.
  • Monocular Attention Windows: Small attention windows localize fragment positions via interactive perception for precise grasping, eliminating the reliance on accurate depth estimation.
  • High Success Rate: Physical experiments on 100 trials achieved 93% success rate for single-fragment removal, and 95% for five-fragment removals with one human intervention, outperforming previous procedural baselines and SOTA policies.
  • Efficient Throughput: Average removal time of 11 seconds per fragment, translating to 304 fragment removals per hour on a cable-driven da Vinci surgical robot.
